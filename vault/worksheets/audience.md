# Worksheet: audience, who the work is for, described by what they did

## When to use it

When "the user" in your [[worksheets/product-brief]] is still vague, or when you are about to decide something
that depends on who will use the result. For school: who reads your work. For a company: who pays and who uses
(often two people). For personal projects: you, or someone you can name.

## What Claude does with it

Claude uses it to judge proposals ("would this person notice?") and to sort pains by whose they are. It does
not invent personas: every line comes from something you observed or were told.

## Fields

| field | question | your answer |
|---|---|---|
| who | A role or a real person you can ask. One line. | |
| what they do today | The steps, in order, for the task your product touches. | |
| what they said | Two verbatim quotes, with dates. | |
| what they pay with | Money, time, attention, or nothing. | |
| who decides | The person who says yes, if different from who uses it. | |
| how to reach them | Where you can ask them a question this week. | |
| who it is NOT for | One kind of person you are deliberately leaving out. | |

## Rules

1. **Observed beats imagined.** If you have not talked to anyone, write "not observed yet": that is the finding.
2. **One audience per sheet.** A second kind of user gets a second copy.
3. **Check it against the pains.** If the pains in [[worksheets/pains]] come from someone not described here, the
   audience is wrong, not the pains.
